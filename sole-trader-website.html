Websites for sole traders and tradespeople

If you run the business on your own and most of your work comes from word of mouth, you do not need a fifteen-page website. You need two or three pages that load instantly on a phone, say what you do and where, and make it easy to ring you. That is £249.

What a site like this actually needs

> Most people who find you are on a phone, often standing in the room with the problem you fix. They want four things fast: what you do, where you cover, whether you look trustworthy, and how to reach you. Everything else is decoration.

> So a Starter build is usually a home page that answers all four above the fold, a services page that lists the work you take on, and a contact page with your number as a tappable link. Photos of your actual work do more for you than any design flourish.

> It also gets the unglamorous things right: a tappable phone number, your coverage area written into the text so local searches can match it, fast loading on a weak mobile signal, and the legal pages the UK requires.

$ cat getting-found.txt

Getting found

> I will be straight with you: a brand new three-page site will not outrank established competitors on a competitive search overnight. Anyone who promises that is selling something.

> What the site does do is give you something solid to point people at, somewhere that is yours rather than a social profile, and a properly structured page that Google can read, index and match to searches mentioning what you do and where you work. Every build is submitted to Google Search Console so indexing starts immediately.

> The biggest single thing you can do alongside it, and it is free, is set up a Google Business Profile so you appear on Maps and in local results. I will point you at it during handover. For most trades that is where the calls come from, with the website as the thing that convinces them once they land.

If you have no photos or text

> That is normal and it is not a blocker. Most people I speak to have a phone full of job photos and nothing written down.

> Send me the photos as they are, even if they are not perfect, and I will crop, compress and place them. For the text, answer a few plain questions about what you do and I will shape it into proper page copy for you to approve. You will not be left staring at an empty document waiting for inspiration.

> If you genuinely have no photos, the site can be built to work without them using clean layout and clear typography, and photos can be dropped in later.
